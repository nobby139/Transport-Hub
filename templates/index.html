1. Parking Too Close Together (Creates a Tight Chicane)

When cars park on both sides of a narrow road, they unintentionally create a tight chicane...

  • Leave at least 1 metre between parked cars on opposite sides of the road.
  • Never park directly opposite another vehicle on a narrow street.
  • Always consider whether a fire engine could get through.

2. Pulling Out in Front of a Bus or Large Vehicle

Many drivers underestimate how long it takes a heavy vehicle to stop...

  • Never pull out unless you can reach full speed safely.
  • Large vehicles need significantly more stopping distance.
  • Passengers can be thrown forward if forced to brake suddenly.

3. Not Understanding Mirror Reach

Bus and large vehicle mirrors extend far beyond the body of the vehicle...

  • Always give buses and large vehicles extra side clearance.
  • Never squeeze past a bus at a stop.
  • Mirror arms can extend 30–50cm beyond the vehicle body.

4. Sitting in a Bus or HGV Blind Spot (The “No‑See Zone”)

Large vehicles have significant blind spots along the sides, behind, and close to the front...

  • Avoid driving directly alongside a bus or HGV.
  • Never sit close behind a large vehicle.
  • If you cannot see the driver’s mirrors, they cannot see you.

5. Cutting Across a Bus or Large Vehicle at a Roundabout

Buses and HGVs need far more time and space to slow down...

  • Never assume a large vehicle can slow down quickly.
  • Give buses and HGVs priority when they are already on the roundabout.
  • Wait for a clear gap before entering.

6. Overtaking a Bus or Large Vehicle While It’s Signalling to Pull Out

When a bus signals to pull out from a stop, drivers must give way...

  • Always allow a bus or large vehicle to pull out when it signals.
  • Do not accelerate to block or overtake.
  • Large vehicles rejoin traffic frequently — be patient.

7. Stopping Too Close Behind a Bus or Large Vehicle at a Stop

Stopping too close behind a bus traps it at the stop...

  • Leave at least a full car length behind a stopped bus or HGV.
  • Allow large vehicles space to angle out safely.
  • Do not block bus stops or laybys.

8. Not Realising How Wide a Bus or Large Vehicle Turns

Buses and HGVs make wide turns. The rear end can swing out significantly...

  • Give large vehicles extra space at junctions and corners.
  • Never stop alongside a bus or HGV that is signalling to turn.
  • Stay well back and allow the manoeuvre to complete.

9. Cutting In Front of a Bus or Large Vehicle at a Stop or Layby

Some drivers accelerate to get ahead and then brake sharply...

  • Never accelerate to get in front of a bus approaching a stop.
  • Allow large vehicles space to slow down and position safely.
  • Buses may have standing passengers who can be thrown forward.

10. Not Understanding Bus Acceleration Delay

Buses and large vehicles do not accelerate as quickly as cars. Their size, weight, and gearing mean they take longer to build speed, especially when pulling away from stops, junctions, or roundabouts. Many drivers misjudge this delay and attempt risky manoeuvres around a bus that is still gathering speed.

  • Large vehicles take longer to accelerate from a standstill.
  • Never assume a bus can match your speed immediately after pulling out.
  • Allow extra space and time when following a bus leaving a stop or junction.

11. Being Forced to Stop by a Driver Who Failed to Give Way

A van continued driving into a narrow section even though the bus was already committed...

  • Never expect a bus or large vehicle to drive onto the pavement.
  • Wait at the wider point and allow the larger vehicle to pass.
  • Large vehicles cannot reverse easily once committed.

12. Cyclists Continuing on Narrow Roads

Cyclists may legally use narrow roads, but the Highway Code expects all road users — including cyclists — to avoid creating unsafe situations. On roads where large vehicles cannot pass safely, cyclists should allow following traffic to overtake when it is safe to do so. This helps prevent pressure, close passes, and unsafe manoeuvres.

  • Large vehicles including buses, HGV's, agricultural machinery, and trailers often require more space to pass safely.
  • When a road becomes too narrow for safe overtaking, all road users should adjust their position or stop when appropriate.
  • Allowing following traffic to pass when safe helps reduce risk and prevents unsafe overtaking attempts.
