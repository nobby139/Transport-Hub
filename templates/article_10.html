Respect and Teamwork on the Road

Transport is a team effort. Even when you’re alone in the cab, you’re part of a wider operation that includes controllers, engineers, cleaners, supervisors, and other drivers. Respect and teamwork keep services running smoothly, reduce stress, and create a safer, more professional environment for everyone.

How you treat colleagues and passengers shapes the culture around you. A respectful, supportive approach builds trust, improves communication, and makes difficult days easier to handle.

Respecting Colleagues and the Job

Respect starts with recognising that everyone has a role to play. From depot staff to controllers, each person is working under their own pressures and responsibilities.

  • Be polite and professional with colleagues, even when you disagree.
  • Avoid blame and gossip — focus on solutions, not fault‑finding.
  • Follow company procedures and local rules consistently.
  • Look after shared spaces: cabs, mess rooms, and facilities.
  • Treat new starters with patience and support, not judgement.

Working as a Team on the Network

Good teamwork keeps the service moving, especially when things go wrong. Clear communication and cooperation between drivers and control can prevent small issues becoming major disruptions.

  • Give clear, accurate information when speaking to control or supervisors.
  • Follow instructions carefully and ask if something is unclear.
  • Share useful information with other drivers about diversions or hazards.
  • Offer practical help when colleagues are under pressure, if it’s safe to do so.
  • Respect handover times and pass on key information about the vehicle or route.

Supporting Each Other on Difficult Days

Every driver has tough shifts — delays, incidents, difficult passengers, or personal stress. A strong team culture means no one feels like they’re dealing with everything alone.

  • Check in on colleagues who’ve had a bad shift or serious incident.
  • Listen without judgement if someone needs to talk.
  • Encourage others to use support services if they’re struggling.
  • Share tips and experience with less‑experienced drivers.
  • Celebrate small wins: a smooth shift, good feedback, or a tricky situation handled well.

Respecting Passengers While Keeping Boundaries

Respect doesn’t mean saying yes to everything. It means treating passengers fairly and calmly, while keeping clear boundaries and following company policy.

  • Speak to passengers in a calm, neutral tone — especially when they’re frustrated.
  • Avoid taking comments personally; focus on the issue, not the emotion.
  • Be clear about what you can and cannot do within the rules.
  • Treat all passengers equally, regardless of background or attitude.
  • Use discretion where appropriate, but never compromise safety or legality.

Professional Communication in the Team

Clear, respectful communication is at the heart of good teamwork. The way you speak on the radio, in the depot, or in messages can either build trust or create tension.

  • Keep radio and phone communication brief, clear, and professional.
  • Avoid sarcasm or jokes that could be misunderstood.
  • Raise concerns through the proper channels, not in public arguments.
  • Give feedback constructively, focusing on behaviour, not personality.
  • Be open to feedback yourself, and see it as a chance to improve.

Looking After Yourself and the Team

Respect and teamwork also mean recognising your own limits. A tired, stressed, or distracted driver is more likely to make mistakes, which affects everyone around them.

  • Use breaks properly — step away from the cab and reset.
  • Ask for support if workload or stress is becoming too much.
  • Be honest about fitness to work, especially after difficult incidents.
  • Encourage a culture where it’s okay to say you’re struggling.
  • Lead by example: calm, consistent, and professional.

Building a Culture You’d Want to Work In

Respect and teamwork aren’t just policies — they’re daily choices. The way you speak, listen, and respond to others shapes the environment you work in. By supporting colleagues, communicating clearly, and treating people fairly, you help build a culture that you’d be proud to be part of.

When drivers look out for each other, share information, and act with respect, the whole operation benefits — from the depot to the road to the passengers on board.
